How I Use AI

A full account of the AI tools I used this semester, how I used them, what they produced, and how I verified, corrected, or rejected their output.

Tools I Used

Across the semester I worked with the following AI tools:

ChatGPT (OpenAI)

Used for brainstorming, prompt drafting, explaining concepts, text generation, and as a thinking partner for reflections. I did not publish ChatGPT text without rewriting it substantially.

DALL·E (OpenAI)

Used for image generation in Makes 2 and 12. I wrote all prompts myself and selected from multiple outputs — the machine generated, I curated and framed.

Canva AI Tools

Used for layout, image generation, and design assistance in Makes 3, 4, 10, and 11. Canva's "Magic" features assisted with visual composition.

Claude (Anthropic)

Used for research assistance, code help for the website, and as a second opinion when I was stuck. All code was reviewed and understood before use.

Limits, Bias, and Ethical Considerations

Bender et al.'s "Stochastic Parrots" (2021) taught me that language models are not neutral — they encode the biases of their training data, amplified at scale. I encountered this directly when DALL·E consistently made my selfie look more European, or ChatGPT described labor conditions in vague, sanitized language].

D'Ignazio and Klein's Data Feminism (2020) pushed me to ask: whose data trained these models, and whose perspectives are missing? When I used ChatGPT to research [topic], I noticed [specific observation about what it got wrong or whose perspective was absent].

I tried to address these limits by:

  • Cross-checking any factual claims AI made against independent sources before including them
  • Noting when AI output felt stereotyped or generic, and revising prompts or rejecting the output
  • Being explicit in attribution boxes about what AI generated vs. what I wrote or decided
  • Never publishing AI-generated text as my own without substantial rewriting and disclosure

Crawford and Joler's "Anatomy of an AI System" (2018) also reminded me that using these tools has a cost beyond the screen — in energy, in labor, in planetary resources. I tried to hold that awareness even when it was uncomfortable.
