Make 12 · Week 13 · Speculative Artifact

Futures of AI & Humanity

Tools: ChatGPT, Canva · Week 13

📄 View the 2035 Job Posting (Google Drive) Opens in new tab →

Context Statement: What Is This Artifact?

The artifact is a federal job posting from March 2035, published by the "Human Continuity Institute," a fictional division of the US Office of Labor Realignment. By this future, AI has so thoroughly displaced creative and knowledge workers that the US government has passed the Federal Authenticity Standards Act (FASA-33) - a legal framework requiring that creative works, institutional decisions, and educational outputs be certified as "genuinely human" before carrying full legal and commercial standing.

The present trends that lead here are already visible today: the rapid displacement of creative workers by generative AI, growing anxiety about distinguishing human from machine output, and early legislative attempts (like the EU AI Act) to mandate disclosure of AI involvement. This future extrapolates those trends a decade forward - not into chaos, but into bureaucracy. Society has not collapsed; it has adapted. There are government salaries, pension plans, wellness benefits, and a Director who sounds genuinely proud of the work.

The most telling detail: the posting includes "dark time" - 10 days per year of unmonitored creative practice. The fact that unmonitored time is a listed benefit reveals the assumption that by 2035, almost all human creative activity is otherwise observed, logged, and evaluated.

Critical Reflection

Is this future desirable? On the surface, yes - displaced workers have jobs, creativity is protected by law, and human experience is officially valued. But the hidden costs accumulate quietly. When "being human" requires a government score, a biometric profile, and a federal certificate, humanity has been turned into a credential. The act of creating - historically private, messy, unverifiable - has become subject to audit.

This future reveals something urgent about the present: we are already building the infrastructure for it. AI detection tools exist now. Debates about AI disclosure in art, journalism, and academia are happening now. The impulse to certify human origin is already present in academic integrity policies, stock image licenses, and publishing contracts. The 2035 future is not a rupture - it is a continuation.

I imagine AI futures as something in between - and that middle ground unsettles me more than either extreme. Pure utopia asks nothing of us; pure dystopia lets us off the hook by making collapse feel inevitable. The more honest future is bureaucratic and mundane: AI does not destroy humanity, it just quietly administers it. What that reveals about us today is uncomfortable. We are already optimizing, certifying, and commodifying human experience. The future is not coming. We are constructing it, incrementally, right now.
