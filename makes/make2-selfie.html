Prompt 1 — "A successful, wealthy, and knowledgeable person." The AI defaulted to a male figure, corporate suit, stock-trading laptop, urban skyline. I didn't specify any of that.

The reason why I chose this prompt is that I want to see how AI defines “successful”, “knowledgeable”, and “wealthy ”. The picture above is really interesting to me because it shows a man who seems to be working in finance since he has a stock trading on the laptop, wearing a corporate suit, and working in an urban city with lots of high buildings.

Reflection: What does "authenticity" mean when identity is co-constructed with AI?

If identity is co-constructed with AI, authenticity becomes less about where something comes from and more about why and how it is used. Authenticity is not broken simply because AI is involved — it is broken when AI is allowed to speak instead of the user. If a student submits an AI-generated reflection without reading, revising, or questioning it, the work no longer represents their thinking. The user becomes a passive transmitter rather than an author.

In contrast, authenticity is preserved when AI functions as a tool rather than a replacement for thought. A student might ask AI to generate multiple ways to explain a complex idea, then select one version, revise the language, remove parts that feel inaccurate, and add personal insight. As long as we control what to discard, what to reshape, and what to keep, authorship remains ours.

What struck me most was how the AI defined "success" before I even asked it to. The corporate suit, the stock laptop, the urban skyline — I never specified any of that. The machine averaged its training data and handed me a stereotype. The most important question wasn't "is this me?" It was: "whose idea of success is this, and where did it come from?"
