Make 4 · Week 5 · Animated GIF

GIFs & Remix Culture: Happy Campus

Tools: Phone camera, ezgif.com, ChatGPT · Week 5

🎞️

GIF file: assets/make4.gif

Process Notes

To make this GIF, I first filmed a short video on campus using my phone. I chose a location that had some movement — people walking, trees, background motion — because I thought repetition would be more noticeable if something was actually changing in the scene. After recording, I uploaded it to ezgif.com to convert the clip into a GIF.

On ezgif, I trimmed the video to select a short moment that could loop smoothly. I decided where the GIF should start and end, adjusted the length, and tested different versions to see how the repetition looked. I focused on making the loop feel continuous so the transition was less obvious. Even though the software automatically generated the frames and looping effect, my decisions shaped the final result and meaning.

Reflection: How does AI alter authorship and remix culture?

Turning a short campus video into a looping GIF changed how I understood the moment. When the video played normally, it simply documented a passing scene. Once it became a loop, the moment felt stretched and exaggerated. Repetition made small movements — like butterfly patterns or background motion — impossible to ignore. Instead of moving forward in time, the GIF traps the viewer in one continuous cycle. This shows how repetition can create meaning by directing attention to specific details.

Looping also changes emotion and perception. After watching the GIF several times, the scene begins to feel less natural and more mechanical, even though it shows a real place. The fifth time watching the loop feels different from the first — patterns become more visible and the moment feels persistent. The GIF flattens the larger context of campus life into a single gesture that keeps repeating.

Automation also played a role. EZGIF automatically processed frames and generated the looping structure. AI and digital tools complicate authorship because they assist production, but they do not decide meaning. The technology creates possibilities; human intention determines what repeats and why. Authorship here is a collaboration between human agency and computational processes.
