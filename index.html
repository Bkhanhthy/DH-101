Being Human
in the Age of AI

I'm Katie Bui — a junior studying Financial Economics and Mathematics. This semester in DH 101: Critical Making, I built, broke, questioned, and remade things alongside AI tools to figure out what machines can — and cannot — do in place of us.

AI can be dangerous because it flattens identity into patterns and probabilities — but my making process revealed what it cannot replace: the human capacity to judge, to feel, and to mean something.

About This Portfolio

What You'll Find Here

Twelve weeks of making, reflecting, and reckoning with what AI can and cannot do.

This portfolio is both an archive and an argument. Across twelve makes — from AI-generated selfies to bots, maps, comics, and speculative futures — I worked with tools like ChatGPT, DALL·E, and Canva to create things I couldn't have made alone. But in every case, the judgment was mine.

Readings by Bender et al., D'Ignazio & Klein, and Crawford & Joler gave me language for what I was experiencing: that AI doesn't just assist us — it shapes us, flattens us, and sometimes obscures the labor and power behind what looks like magic. These pages document how I pushed back.

Each make includes the artifact itself, a reflection connecting it to course concepts, and a full attribution log of every tool and contribution. Nothing is hidden.

Theoretical Grounding

Key Readings

These texts shaped how I think about AI — not as neutral tools, but as systems embedded in power, labor, and ideology.

  • Bender et al., "On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots" (2021) — AI language models risk encoding and amplifying bias at scale
  • D'Ignazio & Klein, Data Feminism (2020) — data is never neutral; whose data counts, and who is rendered invisible?
  • Crawford & Joler, "Anatomy of an AI System" (2018) — every AI interaction sits atop a hidden stack of human labor and planetary resource extraction
  • Ratto, "Critical Making" (2011) — making as a form of inquiry, not just production
  • Sousanis, Unflattening (2015) — against the reduction of human thought to single dimensions
